# Ch 1: Specificity
Book: Scientific Principles of Hypertrophy Training | Authors: Israetel, Hoffmann, Davis, Feather

## Core Principle
Most important training principle. Training must directly cause growth OR potentiate future growth. If you can't explain how training supports your goals, you're violating specificity.

## Two Components

### 1. Directed Adaptation
- Repeated, sequential stimuli → adaptations "stack"
- Same exercises/rep ranges for weeks = best gains
- Switching too frequently = incomplete gains (preparatory → myofibrillar hypertrophy takes weeks)
- Beginners: can be slightly less strict
- Advanced: need very consistent stimulus across weeks

### 2. Training Modality Compatibility
All training competes for recovery resources. More incompatible activities = more interference.

**Compatibility ranking (worst to best for hypertrophy):**
1. Endurance sports (triathlon, distance running)
2. Combat sports (MMA, boxing, BJJ)
3. Team sports (soccer, basketball)
4. Glycolytic sports (400m, 800m)
5. Gymnastics/parkour
6. Strength sports (powerlifting, strongman)
7. Power sports (weightlifting)
8. Technique sports (golf, table tennis)
9. Low-impact hobbies (hiking, yoga)

**Four interference factors:**
1. More overlapping structures used → more interference
2. Less similar stimulus type → more interference
3. More calories required → more interference
4. More disruptive/fatiguing → more interference

## Needs Analysis
Before programming:
1. Assess sport/goal requirements
2. Identify strengths and weaknesses
3. Rank muscle priority (competitors: objective; hobbyists: subjective)
4. Higher priority muscles → earlier in session, more volume, more isolation work

## Diet Note
~1g protein/lb bodyweight daily. Surplus for optimal gains. 4-6 evenly-spaced meals.

## Common Mistakes

**Under-application:**
- Generic programs not tailored to your needs analysis
- "Squats and milk" mentality (one exercise won't grow everything)
- Conflating strength training (3-6 reps) with hypertrophy (higher reps better)
- Working to heavy singles then backdowns wastes growth potential

**Over-application:**
- Beginners doing extreme specialization (not needed; muscles are small)
- Only 1-2 exercises per muscle forever (miss regional hypertrophy, fiber pools)
- Excessive isolation; compounds give more stimulus per set
