# Ch 7: Individualization
Book: Scientific Principles of Hypertrophy Training | Authors: Israetel, Hoffmann, Davis, Feather

## Core Principle
Tailor training principles to individual needs and responses. Start with reasonable plan, let feedback drive adjustments. "In-flight corrections" to the hypertrophy missile.

## Individual Differences

### Work Capacity
Ability to tolerate work at given load/duration. Higher work capacity → higher per-session volumes, but often correlates with higher slow-twitch %.

### Recovery Ability
Rate of recovery varies by person, muscle, system (neuromuscular vs connective tissue). Autoregulation is critical.

### Volume Landmarks
MV, MEV, MAV, MRV all individual. Change daily, weekly, monthly, yearly. Use algorithms to calibrate; don't just guess.

### Genetics Affecting Hypertrophy
- **Adaptive magnitude**: Growth per unit stimulus
- **Anthropometry**: Body proportions affect technique needs
- **Fiber type distribution**: Fast vs slow twitch ratios
- **Growth pathway responses**: Tension vs metabolite sensitivity
- **Initial muscle sizes**: Starting point differences
- **Gain rates**: Speed of adaptation (changes over time)
- **Maximum potential**: Satellite cell pools, cell numbers
- **Muscle shape**: Limited ability to change
- **Intermuscular differences**: Each muscle responds differently
- **Sex**: Females higher MRVs, faster recovery, lower starting size

### Training Age
Closer to career peak → more fine-tuned training needed. Advanced lifters need more precision.

### Biological Age
Younger: faster recovery, less coordination
Older: slower recovery, better focus/MMC

### Lifestyle
Sleep, nutrition, stress massively impact results. Optimize these before obsessing over program details.

## Individualizing Each Principle

### For Specificity
Determine RSM vs SFR vs STR priorities:
- Not time/recovery constrained → focus RSM
- Recovery limited → focus SFR
- Time limited → focus STR
- Optimal balance → focus SFTR

### For Overload
- Calibrate MEV at start of each meso
- Load vs rep progression per exercise (heavy lifts: load progression; light lifts: rep progression)
- Individual responses to ranges

### For Fatigue Management
- Use Set Progression Algorithm
- Note muscle vs joint/connective tissue recovery asymmetries
- Adjust deload intensity if CT lagging

### For SRA
- Derive frequency organically per muscle
- Don't impose generic frequencies

### For Variation
Determine best rep ranges per muscle:
Test each range for 1 block (75% volume in test range). Assess:
- Pump per set
- Disruption per set
- Mind-muscle connection quality
- Rep strength gains

Then bias training toward best-responding ranges.

### For Phase Potentiation
Individual variations:
- Mesocycle loading bias
- Mesocycle length (MEV-to-MRV time)
- Resensitization phase length
- Meso number per block before resensitization
- Nutritional phase length/frequency
- Degree of specialization needed

## By Training Age

### Beginners
- **Specificity**: Learn barbell basics; train whole body
- **Overload**: 5-10 rep range, stay at 2+ RIR
- **Fatigue**: Long accumulations OK; technique > intensity
- **SRA**: 2-3 sessions/week, full body each session
- **Variation**: 1-2 exercises per muscle per year; minimal changes
- **Phase Potentiation**: Blocks can last ~1 year

### Intermediates
- **Specificity**: 65/35 bias toward large muscle groups
- **Overload**: All rep ranges; learn to train to failure
- **Fatigue**: Focus on SFR; shorter accumulations
- **SRA**: 4-6 sessions/week; derive per-muscle frequencies
- **Variation**: 2-3 exercises per meso; individualize technique
- **Phase Potentiation**: More frequent resensitization; specialization begins

### Advanced
- **Specificity**: Focus on weak points; master MMC
- **Overload**: 2-0 RIR can dominate; small set/rep/load jumps
- **Fatigue**: Lighter deloads; more active rest phases
- **SRA**: 6+ sessions possible; may need twice-daily
- **Variation**: Frequent exercise rotation; pre-exhaust; partial ROM OK
- **Phase Potentiation**: Heavy specialization; MV for non-priority muscles

## By Diet Phase

### Muscle Gain (Hypercaloric)
- Lower MEV, higher MRV, lower MV
- MAV curve height increased
- Can extend accumulations by 1+ weeks
- Higher RSM exercises OK
- Slight frequency increase possible

### Weight Maintenance (Eucaloric)
- Baseline adaptive window
- MV training often best (resensitization)
- Low frequency OK
- High RSM exercises fine

### Fat Loss (Hypocaloric)
- Higher MEV, lower MRV, higher MV
- MEV-MRV window compressed
- Keep meso length; reduce load/rep jumps
- Stay near MEV (intermediates+)
- Lighter rep ranges safer as fatigue rises
- Higher SFR exercises essential
- No real specialization possible

## Specialization Phases
When systemic MRV blocks local MRV:
- Train priority muscles MEV→MRV
- Train non-priority muscles at MV (or MEV for intermediates)
- Rotate priorities block to block

Beginners: Don't need
Intermediates: Drop non-priority to MEV
Advanced: Drop non-priority to MV

## Common Mistakes

### Over-Application
- "Special snowflake syndrome" - abandoning exercises that haven't been properly learned
- Modifying technique so much it prevents overload
- Not giving new exercises enough time (need 1+ meso)

### Under-Application
- Copying pro programs wholesale
- Training identically to training partner
- "The grind" - pushing past systemic MRV out of misplaced dedication
- Assuming certain exercises are mandatory for everyone
