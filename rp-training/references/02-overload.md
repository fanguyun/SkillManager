# Ch 2: Overload
Book: Scientific Principles of Hypertrophy Training | Authors: Israetel, Hoffmann, Davis, Feather

## Core Principle
Second most important after Specificity. Training must be challenging enough (acute overload) AND get progressively harder (progressive overload).

## Hypertrophy Stimulators (ranked by importance)
1. **Tension** - force in muscle; main driver
2. **Volume** - sets/reps; most controllable
3. **Relative Effort** - proximity to failure (RIR)
4. **Range of Motion** - stretch under tension matters
5. **Metabolite Accumulation** - "the burn"
6. **Cell Swelling** - "the pump"
7. **Mind-Muscle Connection** - awareness of target muscle
8. **Movement Velocity** - controlled, ~3s each phase
9. **Muscle Damage** - correlated but not causative

## Effective Stimulus Ranges

### Relative Effort
- **5-0 RIR** = effective range
- **2-3 RIR average** = best stimulus:fatigue ratio
- Progress from ~4-5 RIR week 1 → 0-1 RIR final week

### Tension (Load)
- **30%-85% 1RM** = effective range
- Below 30%: insufficient tension even to failure
- Above 85%: diminishing returns, injury risk, fatigue

### Volume
- **MEV → MRV** = effective range
- MEV: ~10 sets/muscle/week typical starting point
- MRV: ~20-25 sets/muscle/week typical ceiling
- Count "hard sets" (5-0 RIR, 5-30 reps, full ROM)
- Sets equate roughly regardless of rep range (5-30 reps)

### Range of Motion
- Full ROM > partial ROM for most situations
- Stretch under tension is independent growth signal
- Exception: partials for targeting specific muscle regions

### Movement Velocity
- Controlled eccentric: 2-3 sec
- Touch-and-go or brief pause
- Controlled concentric: as fast as controlled
- Max ~9 sec total per rep

## Training Methods

### Straight Sets (core of program, 2/3-3/4 of volume)
- Rest 30s-4min based on: target muscle, synergists, CNS, cardio recovery
- Before next set: Can target muscle do 5+ reps? Synergists ready? Mentally ready? Breathing normal?

### Down Sets
- Lighter straight sets after heavier work
- Use when reps drop below target range

### Giant Sets
- Total rep target, any number of sets
- Good for: beginners, time constraints, mind-muscle focus
- Hard to track week-to-week performance

### Supersets
- **Non-overlapping**: Antagonist muscles back-to-back. Time efficient.
- **Pre-exhaust**: Isolation → compound for same muscle. Better targeting.

### Myoreps
- High rep set (10-20) then mini-sets (5-10 reps) with only breaths between
- High effective rep ratio; isolation exercises only
- Fatiguing; use sparingly, end of session

### Drop Sets
- To failure, reduce weight, repeat
- Similar to myoreps; targets slower fibers more
- Isolation exercises, end of session

### Occlusion Training
- Blood flow restriction bands
- 20%+ 1RM effective; good for injury rehab
- Limited to extremities

## Progression

### Load Progression
- Add weight to stay within target RIR
- Err on lighter side; can always add more

### Rep Progression
- Add reps when weight jumps too large
- Stay within target rep range

### Set Progression (most important for hypertrophy)
- Start at MEV (~2-4 sets/session/muscle)
- Add 0-2 sets/week based on recovery
- Use RSM and Set Progression Algorithm

## Raw Stimulus Magnitude (RSM)
Rate 0-3 for each:
1. **Mind-muscle connection**: felt tension/burn in target?
2. **Pump**: cell swelling in target?
3. **Disruption**: fatigue, perturbation, soreness?

Total 4-6 = at MEV, good starting point. Higher = reduce volume. Lower = add volume.

## MEV Estimator
Score 4-6 on RSM after first week = at MEV. Adjust from there.

## Set Progression Algorithm
- Recovering ahead + performance up → add 2+ sets
- Some soreness + performance stable → add 0-1 sets
- Performance declining → employ recovery strategies

## Common Mistakes

### Under-Application
- Resting too short (limiting factor becomes cardio, not muscle)
- Junk volume (not specific, too light, too far from failure)
- Overvaluing mind-muscle connection vs load/volume/effort
- Wrong exercise order (priority muscles trained fatigued)
- Underdosing tension, volume, or relative effort
- Limiting ROM unnecessarily

### Over-Application
- "Heavier is better" mentality
- Over-prioritizing one progression variable
- Beyond-failure training (excessive fatigue, hard to track)
