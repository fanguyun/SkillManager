# Ch 5: Variation
Book: Scientific Principles of Hypertrophy Training | Authors: Israetel, Hoffmann, Davis, Feather

## Core Principle
Change training to prevent adaptive resistance (staleness) and overuse injury. Must stay within Specificity bounds.

## Components
- **Directed Variation**: Changes serve a specific hypertrophy purpose
- **Strategic Variation**: Timing changes to support current + future goals

## Why Vary?
1. **Adaptive Resistance**: Same stimulus becomes less effective over time
2. **Injury Prevention**: Reduce repetitive stress on same structures

## What to Vary

### Exercises
- Compound vs isolation
- Unilateral vs bilateral
- Barbell/dumbbell/machine/cables
- Grip/stance variations

### Movement Velocity
- Cadence (tempo)
- Pauses
- Eccentric emphasis

### Loading (Rep Ranges)
- 5-10 reps (heavy)
- 10-20 reps (moderate)
- 20-30 reps (light)

### Volume
- Train at different landmarks (MV, MEV, MAV, MRV)

### Training Methods
- Straight sets, down sets, drop sets, supersets, myoreps, etc.

### Exercise Order
- Priority placement for target muscles

## Exercise Selection Indices

### RSM (Raw Stimulus Magnitude)
How much growth stimulus per set? Use when recovery/time not limiting.

### SFR (Stimulus to Fatigue Ratio)
Stimulus ÷ fatigue generated. Best when recovery limited.

### STR (Stimulus to Time Ratio)
Stimulus ÷ time required. Best when time limited.

### SFTR (Stimulus Fatigue Time Ratio)
Combines all three. Gold standard for optimal exercise selection.

## When to Replace Exercises
Ask at end of each meso:
1. Performance stalled? No PRs?
2. Causing joint/connective tissue pain?
3. Feeling stale? Poor MMC, weak pumps?

**If mostly "yes"** → replace next meso
**If "yes" only to #2** → replace immediately or fix technique
**If all "no"** → keep the exercise

## Per-Session Exercise Guidelines
- **1-3 exercises/muscle/session** (3 is max)
- **2-4 exercises/muscle/week**
- Average ~3+ sets/exercise/session minimum
- Peak ~15 sets/muscle/session max

## Rep Range Distribution
Starting point for average lifter:
- 25% in 5-10 range
- 50% in 10-20 range
- 25% in 20-30 range

Adjust per individual response and phase.

## Loading by Session/Week
- **Heavy (5-10)**: First in session, earlier in week
- **Moderate (10-20)**: Middle of session/week
- **Light (20-30)**: End of session, later in week

## Movement Velocity Uses
- High axial/systemic fatigue → slow eccentrics (lighter load)
- Greater injury risk → pauses, slow tempo
- Working around injury → controlled movements
- Improving technique → reset with slower cadence

## Volume Variation
- Bias volume toward lower-fatigue exercises during cuts
- Less volume on axially-loading exercises
- More volume on isolation/machine work

## Modality Guidelines
| Method | Best Use |
|--------|----------|
| Straight sets | Core of program, all rep ranges |
| Down sets | After heavy work, stay in rep range |
| Giant sets | Time-limited, MMC focus |
| Supersets | Time efficiency, pre-exhaust |
| Myoreps | End of session, isolation, metabolite |
| Drop sets | End of session, isolation, fatigue |

## Common Mistakes

### Over-Application
- Changing exercises every week (kills momentum)
- Too many exercises/session (warm-up waste, no depth)
- Weekly exercise rotation (loses technique momentum)
- Beginners doing exotic variations
- Wrong exercise order (fatiguing supporting muscles first)
- Mismatching exercise to rep range (deadlifts for 30 reps)

### Under-Application
- Ignoring muscle feel (only chasing load)
- Restricted rep ranges ("8-12 only" or "no cardio reps")
- Never changing stale exercises
- Ignoring joint/connective tissue signals

## Key Numbers
- 1-3 exercises/muscle/session
- 2-4 exercises/muscle/week
- ~3+ sets/exercise average across meso
- ~15 sets/muscle/session max
- Keep same exercises throughout mesocycle (no weekly switching)
