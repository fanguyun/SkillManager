# Ch 3: Fatigue Management
Book: Scientific Principles of Hypertrophy Training | Authors: Israetel, Hoffmann, Davis, Feather

## Core Principle
Third most important. Overload produces both adaptation AND fatigue. Must manage fatigue to continue progress.

## Types of Fatigue

### Local Fatigue
- Muscle-specific disruption, DOMS, weakness
- Connective tissue/joint stress (heals slower than muscle)
- Reduced mind-muscle connection, technique degradation

### Systemic Fatigue
- Whole-body; affects all training
- Sources: physical stress, psychological stress, non-gym stressors
- Signs: lack of motivation, malaise, immune suppression, illness

### Axial Fatigue
- Spinal erector fatigue; technically local but acts systemic
- Limits many lifts (rows → squats both use erectors)

## Fatigue's Effects
- Prevents maximal stimulus (can't train as hard)
- Downregulates anabolic pathways (AMPk ↑, mTOR ↓)
- Hormonal shifts (cortisol ↑, testosterone ↓)
- Injury risk increases

## Stimulus to Fatigue Ratio (SFR)
**SFR = Stimulus / Fatigue**

Higher is better. Central concept uniting Specificity, Overload, and Fatigue Management.

### Stimulus Proxies (0-3 each)
- Mind-muscle connection
- Pump
- Muscle disruption

### Fatigue Proxies (0-3 each)
- Joint/connective tissue disruption
- Perceived exertion
- Unused muscle performance drop

### Best SFR Practices
- Full ROM, controlled tempo
- Target intended muscles, avoid non-target recruitment
- Minimize systemic spillover and axial load
- Good technique = better SFR

### SFR and Volume
- Best SFRs at lower volumes
- But max growth requires MEV → MRV progression
- Accept worse SFR at high volumes for absolute growth

### SFR and RIR
- Best SFR at ~2-4 RIR
- 0 RIR: high stimulus but disproportionate fatigue
- >5 RIR: low fatigue but needs excessive volume

## Fatigue Prevention

### 1. Train Within Volume Landmarks
- Stay below MRV
- When performance drops 2+ sessions, you've hit MRV
- Diet/sleep can't compensate for exceeding MRV

### 2. Safe Technique
- Controlled eccentrics and amortization
- Full ROM (less weight needed, more motor units)
- Stable base (reduces instability fatigue)
- Avoid joint pain (adjust technique if needed)
- Target intended muscles; avoid cheating/momentum

### 3. Microcycle Pulsatility
- Rotate priority muscles across sessions
- Rotate rep ranges (5-10 vs 10-20 vs 20-30)
- Rotate movement types (vertical/horizontal pulling)
- Heavy work early in week; lighter work later
- Place rest days after high-fatigue training

## Fatigue Alleviation

### 4. Rest Days
- 1-2 per week minimum
- Full day off > spread volume across more days
- Reduces psychological fatigue too

### 5. Recovery Sessions
- MV training: half sets, half reps at same weight
- Or: half weight, half sets, normal reps
- Use for: hitting MRV early, suspected minor injury, mild illness
- Return: drop to ~mid MEV-MRV, apply Set Progression Algorithm

### 6. Deloads
- Full week of recovery sessions
- Needed when: multiple muscle groups hit MRV, chronic fatigue
- Protocol:
  - Week 1st half: same weight, half sets, half reps
  - Week 2nd half: half weight, half sets, half reps
- Exercise selection: same as last meso, next meso's exercises, or low-fatigue substitutes

### 7. Active Rest Phases
- 1-4 weeks; typically 1-2 weeks for hypertrophy
- Very minimal or no formal training
- Once per year minimum
- Place after deload, during vacation/holiday

## Pre-Planned vs Autoregulated
- **Pre-planned**: rest days, deloads, active rest
- **Autoregulated**: recovery sessions, early deload triggers
- Use both; autoregulation informs future pre-planning

## Common Mistakes

### Over-Application
- Over-emphasizing recovered state (wastes stimulative time)
- Designing tapers instead of accumulations
- Excessive fear of injury
- Only using perception (not performance) to gauge fatigue

### Under-Application
- Starting too high volume (skip easy gains at MEV)
- Rapid progressions (injury risk, shortened accumulations)
- Exceeding MRV repeatedly (junk volume, plateau, injury)
- Excessive load (>85% 1RM; high fatigue, low extra benefit)
- Low average RIR (going to failure too often)
- Ignoring chronic joint/connective tissue pain

## Key Numbers
- Accumulation phase: typically 3-8 weeks (most: 4-6)
- Rest days: 1-2 per week
- Target RIR average: ~2 (range from 4-5 start → 0-1 end)
- Active rest: 1-2 weeks/year for hypertrophy athletes
