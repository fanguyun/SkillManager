# SBS Glossary

Source: SBS Program Bundle by Greg Nuckols

## Core Concepts

- **TM (Training Max)**: A calculated number used to determine working weights. Not necessarily your true 1RM — may be higher or lower depending on your strength endurance and recovery. TM adjusts automatically each week based on performance.

- **Working Weight**: The actual weight you put on the bar for your work sets. Calculated as `MROUND(TM × week_intensity%, rounding_increment)`.

- **Rounding Increment**: The smallest weight jump available. Typically 5 lb (2.5 lb plates) or 2.5 kg (1.25 kg plates). Set to 0.1 if different exercises need different rounding.

- **1RM (One-Rep Max)**: The maximum weight you can lift for one repetition. Used as the starting point for your TM.

## Autoregulation Terms

- **RIR (Reps in Reserve)**: How many more reps you could have done after completing a set. If you did 8 reps and could have done 10, you had 2 RIR.

- **RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion)**: A scale where RPE 10 = 0 RIR (failure), RPE 9 = 1 RIR, RPE 8 = 2 RIR, etc.

- **RTF (Reps to Failure)**: Taking the last set to absolute failure and counting total reps completed. Used in the RTF and Hypertrophy programs.

- **RIR Cutoff**: In the Set Threshold program — the RIR level at which you stop doing sets. If the cutoff is 3, you stop when you estimate you could only do 3 more reps.

- **RIR Target**: In the Last Set RIR program — the target RIR on your final set. Your TM adjusts based on whether actual RIR exceeds or falls short of the target.

- **RTF Target (Rep Out Target / Last Set Rep Target)**: In RTF programs — the number of reps you aim to hit on the failure set. Beat it and TM goes up; miss it and TM goes down.

- **Set Threshold**: In the Set Threshold program — the range of sets that keeps your TM unchanged. Below the lower threshold = TM decreases. Above the upper threshold = TM increases.

- **Single @8 / Overwarm Single**: An optional heavy single at RPE 8 (2 RIR) performed before work sets. Can be used to update your TM for that session.

## Exercise Categories

- **Main Lifts (Core Lifts)**: The primary barbell movements trained at higher intensity. Default: Squat, Bench Press, Deadlift, OHP (strength) or Block Pulls (hypertrophy).

- **Auxiliary Lifts**: Close barbell variations of main lifts through a similar range of motion. Examples: Paused Squat, Close Grip Bench, Sumo Deadlift, Push Press. Trained at lower intensity than main lifts.

- **Accessory Lifts**: Everything else — rows, pull-ups, single-joint exercises, dumbbell work, machine exercises, unilateral movements. No pre-built progression in most programs.

- **Upper Back Exercises**: Dedicated dropdown slots in every workout. Recommended 2–3x/week minimum. Examples: barbell rows, DB rows, pull-ups, chin-ups, pull-downs, chest-supported rows, T-bar rows.

## Program Structure Terms

- **Block**: A 7-week training phase. The 21-week programs have 3 blocks with increasing intensity.

- **Wave Loading (Weekly Undulating)**: Within each block, intensity increases over 3 weeks, then resets slightly higher for another 3 weeks, followed by a deload.

- **Daily Undulating**: Main lifts are trained at higher intensity than auxiliaries within each session.

- **Deload**: A planned recovery week (weeks 7, 14, 21). Reduced effort — just complete the prescribed sets at the listed weight with no performance tracking.

- **LF (Lower Frequency)**: Program variants where each muscle group is trained fewer times per week. Same overall program structure, different exercise distribution across days.

## Program Builder Terms

- **Classic Overload**: Accessory progression — hit a set/rep goal, then increase load by a percentage.

- **Fixed Number of Reps**: Accessory progression — do N sets to failure aiming for a total rep target across all sets.

- **Set-by-Set / Reverse Pyramid**: Accessory progression — independent rep targets and load progression per set.

- **Deload Toggle**: In the Program Builder, individual cells that mark which weeks are deloads for each exercise.
